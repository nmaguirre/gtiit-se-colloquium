About the Colloquium

A two-day gathering on automated software engineering — from the analysis and generation of code to the formal validation and verification of models and systems.

The GTIIT Colloquium on the Foundations of Modern Software Engineering brings together leading researchers to discuss recent advances in automated software engineering. The event is designed as a forum for graduate and undergraduate students at GTIIT to engage directly with the research community and to witness first-hand how rigour underpins the tools and techniques that modern software engineering relies on.

Topics include automated program analysis, software testing, code and model generation, and the formal validation and verification of software systems. A recurring theme across the colloquium is the deep synergy between software engineering and mathematics: how ideas from logic and formal methods translate into practical tools that help engineers build reliable software.

Attendance is free and open to all GTIIT students and faculty.

Invited Keynote Speakers

Jialun Cao

The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology

Unveiling the Power and Limits of Agentic Workflows in Formal Specification Generation

Abstract & Bio

Abstract

With the rapid development of Large Language Models (LLMs), Reasoning Models, and AI Agents, emerging technologies such as Agentic Workflows, Harnesses, and Skills are reshaping how AI systems tackle complex tasks, while opening up new possibilities for formal specification generation and program verification. This talk explores the evolution from AI-assisted to agentic specification generation, presenting our research and initial explorations into how AI can participate more actively in the formal verification process.

The talk will first review recent approaches to using LLMs to automatically generate formal specifications, and discuss how specifications can be checked and iteratively refined through static analysis, testing, and verifier feedback. We will then introduce our initial explorations of Agent orchestration, Harnesses, Skills, and Jev, investigating how agentic workflows can move beyond one-shot specification generation toward systems that understand the current verification state, select appropriate next actions, and continuously improve specifications based on feedback.

Beyond their potential, we will also examine the limits of agentic workflows in formal specification generation, including challenges in identifying what should be specified, determining whether a generated specification is sufficiently precise and useful, and ensuring that iterative refinement does not lead to overly weak or unintended specifications. Finally, we will discuss the prospects of project-level Formal Verification Agents, and explore how agentic approaches may reshape the workflow of formal verification as AI increasingly takes part in specification generation, planning, and verification decisions.

Bio

Jialun Cao is a Research Assistant Professor at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and will soon join the Department of Computing at Imperial College London as an Assistant Professor. Her research lies at the intersection of AI, Software Engineering and Formal Methods, with papers published at ICSE, FSE, ASE, ISSTA, CAV, ACL, ICML, USENIX Security, and TOSEM. She serves on the program committees of ICSE, FSE, ISSTA, ASE and OOPSLA. Her work has received a Distinguished Paper Award (2024), the ACM SIGSOFT Distinguished Doctoral Dissertation Award (2025), and the Young Scientist Award in Engineering Sciences (2025).

Yepang Liu

Southern University of Science and Technology

Intelligent GUI Testing: An Overview and Our Recent Research

Abstract & Bio

Abstract

Graphical user interfaces (GUIs) provide users with direct access to software functionality. By simulating user interactions, GUI testing validates interface rendering, widget behavior, business workflows, cross-platform compatibility, and UI responsiveness. It plays a crucial role in ensuring a high-quality user experience and supporting regression testing across software releases. However, GUI testing faces substantial challenges in practice, including vast UI state spaces, complex interaction semantics, difficulties in achieving adequate test coverage, and high test script maintenance costs. This talk introduces the basic concepts and core challenges of GUI testing, reviews major technical approaches and representative tools, and presents our recent research on intelligent GUI testing.

Bio

Yepang Liu is a tenured Associate Professor in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering and the Director of the Trustworthy Software Center of the Research Institute of Trustworthy Autonomous Systems at Southern University of Science and Technology (SUSTech). He received his bachelor’s degree from Nanjing University in 2010 and his Ph.D. from the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology in 2015. His research focuses on intelligent software engineering and trustworthy AI systems. He has published 118 papers in international conferences and journals, and has received four ACM SIGSOFT Distinguished Paper/Artifact Awards. The software testing and analysis tools developed by his team have helped uncover more than 2,000 previously unknown defects in open-source projects and have been deployed in large companies such as Huawei and Tencent. He actively serves on the program/organizing committees for leading conferences in software engineering, including ICSE, FSE, ASE, and ISSTA, and on the editorial boards of Empirical Software Engineering, Frontiers of Computer Science, and Journal of Computer Science and Technology.

Sebastian Uchitel

Universidad de Buenos Aires & Imperial College London

Discrete Event Controller Synthesis for Adaptive Systems

Abstract & Bio

Abstract

Adaptation is often defined as the ability of systems to alter at runtime their behaviour in response to changes in their environment, capabilities and goals that were unforeseen at design time. I will discuss why I believe one interesting route to achieving adaptation is to endow systems with the capability of synthesising at runtime discrete event controllers. I will provide an overview of the work we have done in this direction and some of the challenges that remain, covering software architectures, modelling and learning approaches for defining novel discrete event control problems, and algorithms to solve them.

Bio

Sebastian Uchitel is a Professor at Universidad de Buenos Aires (Argentina), Principal Investigator at CONICET (Argentina), holds a Readership at Imperial College London (UK), is an affiliated Researcher at Universidad de San Andrés (Argentina) and a Visiting Professor at the National Institute of Informatics (Japan). His research interests are in behaviour modelling, analysis and synthesis applied to requirements engineering, software architecture and design, and in particular the use of synthesis at runtime to support system adaptation. He was Editor-in-Chief of the IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering and has held editorial responsibilities in Communications of the ACM, the Requirements Engineering Journal, and the Science of Computer Programming Journal. He was program co-chair of ASE’06 and ICSE’10, and General Chair of ICSE’17. He has been distinguished with the Philip Leverhulme Prize, the Konex Foundation Prize and the Houssay Prize, and is a member of the Argentine National Academy of Exact, Physical and Natural Sciences.

Tao Yue

Beihang University

LLMs for Model-Driven Engineering

Abstract & Bio

Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) can support Model-Driven Engineering (MDE) by translating natural language into models and assisting modeling activities. This talk asks why models still matter in the LLM era and reviews the literature and recent advances in using LLMs to support MDE.

Bio

Tao Yue is a full professor at Beihang University. Her main research interests include model-driven engineering, uncertainty-aware software engineering, and quantum software engineering. She has published more than 180 high-quality research papers in the field of software engineering. She is Associate Editor-in-Chief of IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering (TSE) and on the editorial boards of Empirical Software Engineering (EMSE) and Software and System Modeling (SoSyM).

Man Zhang

Beihang University

Modeling Uncertainty with SysML v2

Abstract & Bio

Abstract

Uncertainty is inherent in modern complex systems, particularly cyber-physical and autonomous systems operating in dynamic and unpredictable environments. Model-based systems engineering (MBSE) provides a systematic approach to specifying, analyzing, and understanding such systems. To support reliable system design and analysis, it is essential to explicitly capture and characterize uncertainties within system models. In this talk, we introduce PSUM-SysMLv2, an extension of SysML v2 that integrates the OMG standard Precise Semantics for Uncertainty Modeling (PSUM). We present how uncertainties can be specified and characterized in SysML v2 models, including their sources, types, and potential effects on system behavior. We also introduce a methodology for applying PSUM-SysMLv2 and demonstrate its applicability through case studies.

Bio

Man Zhang is an Associate Professor at Beihang University. Her research focuses on AI-based and model-based approaches to the verification and validation of complex systems, such as cyber-physical systems, autonomous driving systems, and microservices.
