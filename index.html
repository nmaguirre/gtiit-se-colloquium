About the Colloquium

A three-day gathering on automated software engineering — from the analysis and generation of code to the formal validation and verification of models and systems.

The GTIIT Colloquium on the Foundations of Modern Software Engineering brings together leading researchers to discuss recent advances in automated software engineering. The event is designed as a forum for graduate and undergraduate students at GTIIT to engage directly with the research community and to witness first-hand how rigour underpins the tools and techniques that modern software engineering relies on.

Topics include automated program analysis, software testing, code and model generation, and the formal validation and verification of software systems. A recurring theme across the colloquium is the deep synergy between software engineering and mathematics: how ideas from logic and formal methods translate into practical tools that help engineers build reliable software.

Attendance is free and open to all GTIIT students and faculty.

Pre-Colloquium Tutorials

The weekend before the main colloquium — Friday and Saturday, October 23–24 — will feature a series of introductory tutorials aimed at undergraduate and graduate students. No prior background in automated software engineering is assumed.

Tutorials will be delivered by Nazareno Aguirre and Diego Garbervetsky, together with researchers at GTIIT. They are designed to prepare students to engage fully with the colloquium talks and to provide hands-on exposure to the tools and ideas discussed during the main event.

One confirmed tutorial is A Primer on Software Testing, delivered by Nazareno Aguirre, covering the fundamental concepts, methods, and criteria that underlie systematic software testing — from structural and functional coverage criteria to automated test generation.

Additional tutorial topics and the full schedule will be announced.
